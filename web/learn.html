THE KEY IDEA

A change in structure is a reason to look closer.

This monitor compares each eye with its own preflight reference. A flag is not a measurement of how well you can see, a prediction of blindness, or a treatment instruction. The +19.4 μm rule is used here to flag early research signs of optic disc edema. [1, p. 11]

01 / THE MEASUREMENT

OCT looks through layers, not just at the surface.

Optical coherence tomography (OCT) uses light returned from tissue and a reference light path to resolve depth. A cross-sectional image is called a B-scan. Think of a slice through the back of the eye, rather than an ordinary photograph. [4]

The retina is the light-sensing tissue at the back of the eye. Nerve fibres converge at the optic disc, where the optic nerve leaves the eye. This monitor uses total retinal thickness (TRT) near that disc. NASA's report explains that early swelling can be too close to the disc for a conventional, more distant nerve-fibre circle scan to detect. [1, p. 11]

250 μm is the width of the ring, not the retinal thickness. Enter the quality-checked mean TRT for this ring, not macular thickness, RNFL thickness, MRW, or a single-point measurement. One micrometre (μm) is one thousandth of a millimetre.

What do RNFL and MRW mean?

RNFL is the retinal nerve fibre layer, one part of the retina. MRW is minimum rim width: the shortest distance from BMO to the inner retinal surface. They measure different structures; this monitor does not apply the TRT threshold to either. [2]

02 / FROM SCAN TO NUMBER

The research measurement workflow

This explains the published study, not a device operating procedure. Use your mission-approved training and medical team's instructions to acquire scans.

  1. Acquire a matching reference. Macias 2020 used Spectralis OCT (Heidelberg Engineering) on the ground and ISS. Preflight scans were sent to the ISS for follow-up alignment.
  2. Scan both eyes. The study acquired high-resolution radial scans centred on the optic nerve head: 20°, 12 B-scans, ART16. It also acquired 12° circular scans with ART100. ART refers to automatic real-time tracking.
  3. Check the boundaries. Analysts manually corrected automated ILM/BM segmentation and marked BMO. They fitted an ellipse and interpolated the radial thickness map to calculate regional TRT. Circular scans were used for choroidal thickness analysis.
  4. Enter the reviewed result here. Select the correct eye, actual Flight Day, and BMO–250 μm mean TRT. Confirm comparable methods and measurement quality before saving.

Study procedure: [2, Methods]. The last step is this app's workflow.

When were scans taken in the study?

Targets were FD10, 30, 60, 90, 120 and 150, within scheduling windows. A 10-day window does not mean a scan every 10 days. Follow your assigned schedule; the app's daily check-in is not a NASA sampling requirement. [2]

03 / UNDERSTANDING +19.4 μm

A change threshold, not an absolute thickness limit.

Fictional preflight baseline 400 μm→Current measurement 420 μm=Change from baseline +20 μm

Here, +20 exceeds +19.4. At exactly +19.4, this app does not flag an exceedance. Without a baseline, the app cannot assess this threshold.

Where did 19.4 come from?

Laurie's precision study used seven astronauts who had not flown, measured on two visits in different postures and assessed by different readers. Its model estimated a variability SD of 9.7 μm, excluding differences between people. Twice that value is 19.4 μm. Under its normal-distribution assumption, approximately 95% of non-edema-related test–retest variation falls within ±2 SD. The authors interpreted an increase beyond +19.4 μm as an early sign of ODE. [3, Precision Testing]

This does not mean a 95% probability that you have SANS. It is not a population-mean confidence interval, a severity grade, or a threshold for irreversible damage. The research method and measurement region matter.

NASA's 2022 report uses this threshold for early detection and increased monitoring. It does not establish a vision-loss threshold. [1, pp. 11, 41, 51]

04 / WHY MONITOR IT?

ODE is swelling at the optic disc.

Optic disc edema (ODE) is one finding within spaceflight-associated neuro-ocular syndrome (SANS). SANS can also involve flattening at the back of the eye, folds in retinal/choroidal tissue, and a shift toward farsightedness. A TRT value cannot assess all of these. [1, pp. 7, 11–15]

Altered gravity shifts fluid toward the head. How fluid, tissue anatomy and other factors produce SANS is still under investigation; this app cannot identify the cause or measure pressure inside the skull. [1, p. 7]

The concern is persistent swelling.

The 2022 report describes a potential risk that prolonged ODE could damage retinal nerve fibres and cause uncorrectable vision loss on longer missions. It states that this outcome had not been observed at the time and that the duration or magnitude needed to cause damage remained unknown. Crossing +19.4 μm does not establish that damage has happened. [1, pp. 41–42]

The report also distinguishes structural changes from visual function. Vision tests, including visual acuity and visual fields, provide information this thickness tracker cannot. Recovery varies; a decreasing TRT does not prove complete recovery. [1, pp. 12, 49]

05 / PUT THE RESULT IN CONTEXT

What should I do with a flag?

The checklist below is this prototype's review workflow, not a NASA treatment or emergency protocol.

  1. Check the record. Confirm the eye, units, actual measurement day, preflight baseline and correct ring. Check for typing errors.
  2. Check the scan review. Ask the trained reviewer to verify alignment, image quality and tissue boundaries. This app only receives a number; it cannot inspect an OCT scan.
  3. Discuss the finding with your medical team. Share the trend and original scan. NASA's 2022 report associates early-threshold exceedance with increased monitoring; the medical team determines follow-up and whether repeat imaging or other tests are needed. [1, p. 11]

Below threshold? This does not rule out SANS or explain a new visual problem. Follow your mission's medical reporting procedure for visual changes regardless of the number.

No baseline? Interval changes remain available, but the baseline comparison is unknown. Do not substitute a group average or invent a baseline.

What about rate, accumulated deviation and prediction?

In this app, rate averages change between two measurements. Accumulated deviation uses signed trapezoids starting at the first recorded measurement; negative values offset positive ones. Neither is a validated damage score. The optional forecast assumes the latest rate continues; it cannot predict individual visual outcomes. Experimental rate flags request measurement review, not treatment.

06 / RESEARCHED COUNTERMEASURES

Approaches under study—not proven prevention or treatment.

NASA research has explored ways to counter headward fluid shifts and other proposed contributors to SANS. The mechanisms below are research rationales, not instructions or evidence that a measure prevents SANS in an individual. [5–8]

  • LBNP · lower-body negative pressureWhy / principle: negative pressure around the lower body is intended to draw fluid away from the head and toward the legs. Method / equipment: a sealed lower-body chamber connected to a pressure system; flexible suit concepts are also being studied. Use only a mission-approved system and protocol. [7]
  • VTC · venoconstrictive thigh cuffsWhy / principle: thigh cuffs partially restrict venous return, temporarily pooling blood and fluid in the legs below the cuffs. Method / equipment: fitted cuffs around both thighs, with fit and pressure set by the approved research/medical protocol—not ordinary compression garments. NASA researchers have published a planned ISS study and tested cuffs in bed-rest analogs. [6, 7]
  • ITD · impedance-threshold deviceWhy / principle: breathing against inspiratory resistance creates negative pressure in the chest, intended to reduce venous congestion toward the head. Method / equipment: a breathing device with a resistance/threshold valve and mouthpiece. NASA's early study found posture-dependent, difficult-to-interpret responses; effectiveness is not established. [5]
  • MedicationWhy / principle: this log category does not identify a NASA-validated SANS-preventive drug. Method / equipment: record only a medication and dose/timing prescribed or approved by the flight medical team; this guide recommends no medication.
  • SupplementWhy / principle: NASA-reviewed research has considered B-vitamin status/supplementation as a candidate based on observed associations and proposed metabolic pathways; this is not proof that supplements prevent SANS. Method / equipment: if prescribed, record the actual product, route, dose and frequency; do not self-start or change supplements. [8]
  • Other · named interventionWhy / principle: this is a user-defined logging category, not one standardized countermeasure. NASA reviews also discuss approaches such as pressure goggles and artificial gravity as research candidates. Method / equipment: name the actual intervention and equipment in the record and follow its approved protocol; this guide does not recommend using one. [8]
Research status matters.

Some studies test mechanisms or tolerability in Earth-based analogs; they do not establish clinical prevention, treatment, dose, or an individual response in space. Never start, stop, or change a medication, supplement, cuff, pressure device, or breathing device based on this tracker. Ask the mission medical team.

The app pairs logged sessions with nearby OCT scans for observation only. A before/after change cannot establish that a countermeasure caused the change—other sessions, timing, measurement variability and the natural trend may contribute.

EVIDENCE & TRANSPARENCY

Follow the explanation back to its source.

This guide uses the specified historical evidence base. It is not a claim to represent the latest clinical guidance, and it is not a complete review of every paper in the project handoff.

  1. NASA — SANS Evidence Report (2022)

    Risk and interpretation: pp. 7, 11–13, 41–42, 49, 51–52. Verified against a local copy of the 66-page PDF; the online PDF endpoint could not be retrieved during this update. These pages were reviewed, not the complete report.

  2. Macias et al. (2020) — Association of Long-Duration Spaceflight With Anterior and Posterior Ocular Structure Changes in Astronauts and Their Recovery

    Methods and Figure 1 description: equipment, acquisition and regional analysis. Full-text HTML accessible; this is not the source of the 19.4 μm cutoff.

  3. Laurie et al. (2020) — Optic Disc Edema and Choroidal Engorgement in Astronauts During Spaceflight and Individuals Exposed to Bed Rest

    Precision Testing: statistical basis of the threshold. Full-text HTML and local paper PDF accessible.

  4. Nordmann — OCT & Optic nerve (hosted by ZEISS)

    General principles, printed pp. 16–18: light interference and depth imaging. Used for the brief optical principle, not for the Spectralis operating procedure.

  5. NASA NTRS — Use of Otoacoustic Emission Phase Change to Evaluate Countermeasures for SANS (2020)

    NASA-supported poster on LBNP, ITD and VTC, individually and in combination. Read for the device rationale and reported measurement limitations; it does not establish clinical prevention.

  6. NASA NTRS — Mitigating Headward Fluid Shifts with Venoconstrictive Thigh Cuffs during Spaceflight (2021 study design)

    Study plan for fit-checked thigh cuffs during ISS missions. This is a protocol description, not an efficacy result.

  7. Moestl et al. (2025) — Mechanical countermeasures during 30-day head-down tilt bed rest

    Joint NASA–DLR study-methods/tolerability report on LBNP and thigh cuffs in an Earth-based analog; not proof of effectiveness in flight.

  8. Ong et al. (2023) — SANS: Proposed Pathogenesis, Terrestrial Analogs, and Emerging Countermeasures (NASA NTRS)

    NASA NTRS accepted manuscript. Inspected the sections on LBNP, nutritional supplementation, and other emerging strategies; these are candidates under investigation, not approved prevention or treatment.
